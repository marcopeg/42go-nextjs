# Clarify Round v11 — Add Multi-App In-App Notifications and Admin Management

## Questions

### 1. Which top-level backoffice URL namespace should be introduced?

Common options:

- `/backoffice/notifications` — matches the existing `backoffice` role,
  clearly distinguishes internal app operations, and scales naturally to
  `/backoffice/users`. It is longer but unambiguous.
- `/admin/notifications` — shortest and most common externally, but “admin”
  can imply a global superuser and does not match this repository's role name.
- `/manage/notifications` — role-neutral and action-oriented, but less
  conventional and weaker as a durable product-area name.
- `/notifications/manage` — smallest change and keeps the feature together,
  but does not establish the requested shared top-level backoffice namespace.

Recommendation: `/backoffice/notifications`.

### 2. Should the backoffice API mirror that namespace?

Recommendation: use `/api/backoffice/notifications`, while user APIs remain
under `/api/notifications`. Both explicitly require `api:notifications`;
backoffice handlers additionally require the `backoffice` role and their exact
notification grant. A shared URL prefix never replaces handler-level policy.

### 3. Keep users migration and the `users:edit` fix as separate work?

Recommendation: yes, as two focused follow-ups. Moving `/users` and
`/api/users` under the new namespace needs redirects, menu/link updates,
bookmarks, policy tests, and compatibility decisions. Fixing PATCH authorization
also needs consistent `users:edit` grant creation/assignment, client capability
gating, and tests; changing only the handler would break current backoffice
editors in apps where the grant is not assigned.

## Answers

### 1.

Use `/backoffice` as the new top-level namespace. The notification management
page is `/backoffice/notifications` under the authenticated `(app)` route
organization.

### 2.

Mirror the namespace at `/api/backoffice/notifications`. Keep user APIs at
`/api/notifications`. Both explicitly require `api:notifications`; every
backoffice handler additionally requires session, app scope, role
`backoffice`, and the exact notification-management grant.

### 3.

Keep both concerns out of OE92 and draft two dedicated follow-ups: one migrates
the user page/details and APIs into the backoffice namespace with compatibility
handling, and one repairs `users:edit` authorization across API, seeds/role
assignment, client capability gating, and tests.

## Resolution Summary

- Backoffice notification routes use `/backoffice/notifications` and
  `/api/backoffice/notifications`.
- User-route migration and `users:edit` enforcement are separate backlog tasks.
