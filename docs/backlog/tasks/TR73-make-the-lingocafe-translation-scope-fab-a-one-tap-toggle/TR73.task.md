# Make the LingoCafe Translation-Scope FAB a One-Tap Toggle

## Elevator's Pitch

Replace the two-step expandable word-versus-sentence translation FAB with a compact, switch-like floating control. It keeps the established FAB size, sticky placement, safe-area behavior, and thumb reach, but shows the active translation scope directly and toggles to the other scope in one press.

## Business Gain

- Readers can change translation granularity at the moment they need it, with one decisive tap instead of opening a menu and selecting an option.
- The current state becomes visible before a reader touches content, reducing accidental word-versus-sentence translations.

## Current State

- The shared LingoCafe translation-scope control is an expandable FAB. Its first activation only opens separate `Translate sentence` and `Translate word` actions; a second activation selects the desired scope.
- The control is used in the Conversation reader action bar and the Book Reader floating action bar, and it updates the canonical locally persisted reader translation-scope preference.
- The collapsed translation icon alone does not make the active word or sentence scope clear.

## Desired State

- Use one directly actionable, switch-like translation-scope FAB wherever the floating translation control is exposed. Each click or tap immediately toggles between sentence and word scope; no expansion, menu selection, hover dwell, or confirmation step is required.
- Keep the control FAB-like as a compact 56 px-high pill that can legibly contain the translation icon and active-mode label. Preserve its floating/sticky placement, transparent action-bar integration, safe-area offsets, and mobile thumb reach.
- Give its two visual states an unambiguous in-control indication of the current mode: retain the translation icon and visibly label the active scope as `word` or `sentence`. The indication must be legible at the FAB size and fit LingoCafe themes.
- On mobile, changing scope updates only the in-control label. Animate the replacement label with a compact upward rolling transition; do not show a toast or separate confirmation message. Respect reduced-motion preferences by updating the label without the rolling animation.
- On desktop, provide a hover/focus popover that explains the direct action in the form `Click here to switch translation mode to …`, naming the mode that the next activation will select.
- Use the existing canonical translation-scope preference and synchronization flow, so the Book Reader, Conversations, and Reading Preferences panel update immediately and remain in sync after reload.

## Definition of Success

A reader looking at either LingoCafe reading surface can tell whether taps translate sentences or words, then switch to the other mode with exactly one press of the floating FAB. The control stays as reliably placed and easy to reach as the current FAB, but its menu has been sent packing.

## Additional Context

- The supplied mobile reference points to the bottom-left translation FAB in a conversation reading surface. The right-side voiceover FAB and the surrounding floating action-bar geometry establish the size and positioning to preserve.
- Relevant implementation areas include `ConversationActionFab`, `BookReaderFloatingActionBar`, the reusable `ExpandableFab` usage, and the canonical reader translation-scope preference/event flow.
- LI48 deliberately introduced the current two-step expandable translation selector. This task supersedes that interaction choice only for the translation-scope control; direct-action and ordinary menu capabilities of the reusable FAB may still be needed elsewhere.

## Assumptions

- The one-tap control replaces every user-facing floating sentence/word translation-scope FAB in both Conversations and Book Reader; it is not limited to the pictured conversation surface.
- Sentence translation remains the default for missing, invalid, and legacy stored preferences.
- The full Reading Preferences panel continues to expose its existing explicit sentence/word choice; this task changes the fast floating control, not the underlying preference semantics.
- Changing scope applies immediately to subsequent translation interactions and does not alter an already-open translation result.

## Constraints

- Preserve one canonical locally persisted translation-scope value and its existing sanitization, storage, event, and cross-surface synchronization behavior. Do not create a second preference or duplicate state.
- Keep the compact 56 px-high pill independently reachable, fixed or sticky as it is today, responsive, safe-area aware, and free of horizontal overflow near a 390 px viewport.
- Preserve the idle action-bar behavior, translation availability gates, voiceover gating, active playback controls, translation authorization, content validation, caching, highlights, and popover behavior.
- The direct toggle must use semantic button behavior, a precise accessible name that announces the current scope and the effect of activation, keyboard operation, and visible focus treatment.
- The desktop explanatory popover must be available on hover and keyboard focus, identify the mode the next activation will select, and not add a confirmation step or obscure adjacent reader controls.
- Do not turn the compact pill FAB into a persistent full-width segmented control, a rectangular toolbar, or an overlay panel. Do not make users open a menu to change scope.
- Respect light, dark, and system reader themes using established semantic tokens; do not hard-code an isolated color pair.
- The label-change animation must be brief, must not move the control's anchored geometry, and must be disabled for `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- Avoid removing or weakening generic `ExpandableFab` menu behavior unless a verified refactor shows it is no longer used or needed.

## Acceptance Criteria

- The floating translation-scope control toggles sentence and word mode on every single click, tap, Enter, or Space activation.
- No translation-scope menu, expanded action cluster, hover-open interaction, delayed selection feedback, or second activation is required to change scope.
- Before activation, the FAB clearly indicates whether sentence or word translation is currently active through a compact visible in-control state treatment.
- The control retains the translation icon and visibly labels the active scope exactly `word` or `sentence`.
- After activation, the visual and semantic current-mode indication updates immediately to the newly active scope.
- The active-mode label changes with a brief upward rolling transition on mobile, without a toast or other confirmation message; reduced-motion users receive the immediate label update without the animation.
- The control is a compact 56 px-high floating pill that fits the translation icon and active-mode label, while preserving existing action-bar placement, stickiness, safe-area spacing, and touch reach on mobile.
- The control remains visually coherent with the adjacent voiceover FAB and does not shift or obscure it when translation or voiceover capability gates change.
- Book Reader and Conversations use the same canonical translation-scope value and both update correctly after a toggle.
- The Reading Preferences panel reflects a FAB toggle immediately, and a panel change updates the floating control immediately.
- The selected scope persists through the existing local reader-preference mechanism and restores correctly after reload; invalid or legacy values still resolve to sentence mode.
- The control is keyboard operable, exposes its current mode and next-toggle action accessibly, and has a visible focus state.
- On desktop hover and keyboard focus, a popover explains `Click here to switch translation mode to …`, with the target mode named accurately.
- Light, dark, and system reader themes keep the active-mode indication readable and distinguishable.
- Existing sentence and word translation behavior, selected-text highlighting, translation popovers, playback state transitions, and unavailable-feature behavior do not regress.
- Checks near 390 px and desktop width confirm no horizontal overflow, safe-area-safe placement, and legible mode indication.
- `npm run qa` passes.

## Dos

- Do make the currently active scope understandable at a glance before interaction.
- Do preserve the familiar floating control's 56 px height, lower-screen placement, and resilient safe-area behavior.
- Do reuse the existing translation preference controller and synchronization events.
- Do verify both the Conversation and Book Reader surfaces.

## Don'ts

- Do not require a menu open plus a second selection to switch translation scope.
- Do not infer the active scope only from the next action, a tooltip, or an accessibility-only label.
- Do not replace the compact floating pill FAB with a wide persistent toggle bar or relocate it away from its established action-bar slot.
- Do not change translation scope semantics, server persistence, authorization, cache contracts, or voiceover behavior.
