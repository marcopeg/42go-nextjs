# Expose Listening Options in the Reader Customization Panel

## Elevator's Pitch

Extend the LingoCafe page-reading customization panel with capability-aware `Reading` and `Listening` tabs. The Listening tab exposes the same playback preferences available from the playback bar—including speed and text-visibility options—without removing or relocating the original controls.

## Business Gain

Make listening customization discoverable before or during playback. Readers can configure the experience from the persistent page customization icon or from the active playback bar, while both surfaces stay synchronized and control one set of preferences.

## Current State

The page customization icon opens the `BookReaderPreferencesPanel`, currently titled `Reading Preferences`. It contains reader appearance and translation settings only. It has no top-level Reading/Listening navigation and receives no playback controller or capability state.

The playback bar has a separate settings popover backed by `ReaderPlaybackController.preferences`. That popover exposes speed, word highlighting, sentence highlighting, translation auto-pause, settings auto-pause, `Reveal as you listen`, and `Dim previous sentences`. CE70 completed the two focus-mode preferences, and QN48 completed playback actions in the translation popover.

Playback availability is represented by `playback.canPlay`, based on device speech support, a matching language voice, and readable sentences on the page. The playback controller initially reports `Checking device voices...`, subscribes to `voiceschanged`, and resolves `canPlay` after checking the device provider. When playback is unavailable, the playback FAB is hidden. Voice discovery currently belongs to the reader playback hook rather than an app-boot capability service.

`Auto-pause on settings` currently applies only to the playback-bar settings popover. Opening the reader preferences panel or table-of-contents/book-navigation panel does not participate in that pause reason.

## Desired State

When listening is available on the current device and reader context, the page customization panel shows an accessible top-level tab menu near the top with two choices: `Reading` and `Listening`.

### Reading tab

The Reading tab contains the existing customization experience: preview, theme, font size, font family, background, text color, translation scope, Done action, reset behavior, and local-storage notice. Existing reading settings and their behavior remain intact.

### Listening tab

The Listening tab exposes the same playback settings and current values as the settings popover opened from the playback bar:

- playback speed;
- word highlighting;
- sentence highlighting;
- automatic pause on translation;
- automatic pause on playback settings;
- `Reveal as you listen`;
- `Dim previous sentences`.

This task adds a second control surface; it does not move, remove, fork, or replace the playback-bar settings popover. Both surfaces read and update the same `ReaderPlaybackController` preference state. A change made in either location applies immediately, persists through the existing playback-preference store, and is reflected by the other surface the next time it renders or while both are mounted.

### Capability-aware navigation

Use the full existing `playback.canPlay` result as the sole gate for offering Listening. Do not introduce a broader device-only or language-only capability for this panel.

Begin device voice discovery at the earliest LingoCafe client-app boot boundary rather than lazily when the settings panel opens. The customization panel consumes the canonical playback capability and its explicit pending/resolved state; it does not probe speech synthesis independently. If capability is still pending when the panel opens, render a small loading state in the panel until it resolves. Do not briefly render the reading-only fallback while discovery is pending.

Once the open panel has exposed the Listening tab, do not remove it or force the user back to Reading if live capability later becomes unavailable. The user may continue editing stored playback preferences while the panel remains open. The actual playback surface continues to enforce the current real capability. Re-evaluate tab availability the next time the preferences panel opens.

If the resolved full `playback.canPlay` value is false when the panel opens, render the existing reading customization experience without tab navigation and without an empty, disabled, or teasing Listening tab.

### Tab lifecycle and presentation

Every preferences-panel opening starts on Reading. Tab selection is not retained across closes, page navigation, or browser sessions.

The Reading tab keeps the existing live appearance preview. The Listening tab has no preview. It follows the page settings panel's roomier visual composition while reusing the same generic tabs, switches, speed controls, preference bindings, labels, and update logic as the playback popover. Reuse does not require both surfaces to have identical compact layouts.

### Reader-panel auto-pause

Opening the preferences panel participates in the existing `Auto-pause on settings` behavior before the user selects a tab. Switching between Reading and Listening does not add another pause transition. Opening the table-of-contents/book-navigation panel also participates in the same settings pause reason.

When `Auto-pause on settings` is enabled, either reader-level panel pauses active playback and closing the panel resumes only playback that panel auto-paused, following the existing playback-settings popover contract. When the preference is disabled, opening or closing these panels does not pause or resume playback. Translation interactions remain governed separately by `Auto-pause on translation`.

## Definition of Success

On a listening-capable reader, the page customization panel opens on Reading and offers Reading and Listening tabs. The Listening tab changes exactly the same playback preferences as the playback-bar popover, with instant synchronization and persistence. Pending capability produces a contained loader, while an incapable device or reader context gets the current reading-only customization panel with no listening tab. Opening reader preferences or book navigation respects `Auto-pause on settings` without confusing it with translation auto-pause.

## Additional Context

CE70 and QN48 were explicit implementation prerequisites requested by the operator. Both are completed, so this task consumes their final playback-option model, labels, behavior, and capability integration.

WX99 established the existing playback settings popover and local playback-preference controller. The page route already owns both the reader customization state and the playback controller, so it is the natural composition boundary for passing canonical playback settings and availability into the customization panel.

The strongest implementation shape is a shared presentational playback-settings editor used by both the playback-bar popover and the Listening tab. The exact component boundary belongs to refinement/planning, but duplicated option lists, defaults, sanitization, or update logic are not acceptable.

## Assumptions

- Reading is selected on every panel opening; tab selection is never remembered.
- If listening is unavailable, the customization panel retains its current Reading Preferences behavior and does not show a single redundant Reading tab.
- The Listening tab configures playback but does not itself start playback unless refinement explicitly adds that behavior.
- All listening-option changes apply immediately without a Save action.
- The playback bar and customization panel use the same device-local playback preference store.
- The Listening tab uses CE70's final `Reveal as you listen` and `Dim previous sentences` labels and behaviors.
- Existing playback options remain available from the playback bar after this task.
- The capability shown by an already open panel may remain stable until close even when the live playback capability changes; authorization and actual playback availability are not inferred from that UI snapshot.

## Constraints

- Consume the completed CE70 and QN48 contracts without redefining their settings or playback actions.
- Do not move or remove playback settings from the playback-bar popover.
- Do not create separate listening preferences, duplicate storage keys, or synchronize two independent state stores.
- Use one canonical playback preference model, defaults, sanitization flow, setters, and persistence path.
- Reuse the canonical playback capability state; do not add a second speech-synthesis or voice-detection probe inside the customization panel.
- Expose an explicit pending capability state so the panel can distinguish unresolved voice discovery from resolved unavailability.
- Start voice discovery at the earliest suitable LingoCafe client boot boundary rather than in response to opening settings.
- Do not show the Reading/Listening tab menu or a disabled Listening tab when listening is unavailable.
- Preserve the current reading customization controls, preview, reset semantics, modal behavior, and mobile/desktop layouts.
- Keep the top-level tabs keyboard accessible with accurate tab, selected, and panel semantics.
- Ensure both settings surfaces use the same final option set and labels, including CE70’s text-visibility options.
- Keep translation pause behavior under `Auto-pause on translation`; extending `Auto-pause on settings` to preferences and book navigation must not merge the two policies.
- Changing options from the customization panel must preserve active playback position, speech sequencing, highlighting, guided scrolling, translation behavior, and analytics according to each option’s existing contract.
- Run `npm run qa` and visually verify capable and incapable states on mobile and desktop.
