# Add Progressive Reveal and Played-Text Dimming to Reader Playback

## Elevator's Pitch

Add two independent focus options to the LingoCafe playback settings popover: one progressively reveals the book only through the sentence currently being played, and the other dims sentences that playback has already passed. Used together, they keep the active sentence at the visual frontier while reducing distraction from both future and completed text.

## Business Gain

Give language learners a focused read-along mode that synchronizes what they can see with what they hear. Progressive reveal discourages reading ahead, while played-text dimming makes the current spoken sentence easier to find without removing context.

## Current State

The floating playback bar has a gear-and-speed trigger that opens the local Playback settings popover. The popover offers speed, word highlighting, sentence highlighting, and two auto-pause settings. These preferences apply immediately and persist locally.

During playback, the reader exposes the active sentence and optionally highlights the active sentence and word. All earlier and later page content remains fully visible and keeps its normal contrast. There is no progressive reveal or styling for sentences that playback has already passed.

## Desired State

Add two independent boolean options to the existing Playback settings popover:

- `Reveal as you listen`
- `Dim previous sentences`

Both options default to off, apply immediately, and persist in the established device-local playback-preference record.

### Progressive reveal

While `Reveal as you listen` is enabled and playback is open with an active sentence, the active sentence defines a movable visual frontier. Every sentence before the active sentence remains visible, the active sentence remains visible, and everything after it in reading order is visually hidden while preserving its normal layout space. Future content includes later sentence text and later non-sentence structure such as headings, summaries, separators, illustrations, and metadata. If the current sentence shares a block with later sentences, the current sentence remains visible while the later content is hidden.

Sentence state is positional. When playback starts or seeks into the middle of a page, every sentence before the active playback index is immediately considered revealed, even if it was not heard during the current session. Forward playback or seeking reveals through the new active sentence. Backward seeking recomputes the frontier from the new active sentence, so later sentences and content become hidden again.

Playing keeps the frontier synchronized to the active sentence. Pausing, including automatic pauses, freezes the same presentation. Resuming continues from that frontier. Completing playback, closing playback, losing playback availability, or entering a playback error state restores the complete page. A page transition during continuous playback initializes the new page from its active sentence using the same positional rule.

Hidden future content uses CSS visibility so it remains absent from pointer interaction, keyboard navigation, and the accessibility tree while its layout geometry stays identical to the complete page.

Progressive reveal uses the existing centered-sentence guided-scroll path. Because hidden content preserves the complete page geometry, enabling, advancing, seeking, or disabling the option does not introduce a second scroll owner or change scroll measurements.

### Played-text dimming

While `Dim previous sentences` is enabled and playback is open with an active sentence, every sentence before the active playback index uses a theme-aware muted treatment at roughly 50–60% of normal visual prominence. The active sentence stays at normal contrast, and existing active sentence, active word, and translation selection treatments remain legible and unchanged. Exact tokens or opacity may be tuned during light- and dark-theme visual verification.

Dimming uses the same positional rule as progressive reveal rather than tracking which sentences were actually completed during the current session. Starting or seeking into the middle immediately dims every earlier sentence. Seeking backward recomputes the set, restoring normal contrast to sentences that are no longer before the active position. Completing, closing, losing availability, or failing playback removes all playback dimming.

### Combined behavior

When both options are enabled, earlier/played sentences remain visible but dimmed, the active sentence remains fully visible and emphasized by the existing playback highlight settings, and later/unplayed sentences remain hidden until playback reaches them. The two options stay independent: either can be used without the other.

Both settings belong in the existing playback settings popover opened from the gear-and-speed button. Changes apply immediately, use the established local playback-preference storage and sanitization flow, and must not alter speech sequencing, playback position, translation data, analytics, or normal reader progress.

## Definition of Success

A reader can enable either or both focus options and follow playback with the current sentence visually dominant. Progressive reveal exposes one new sentence when playback reaches it; played-text dimming de-emphasizes prior sentences without removing them. Turning the options off restores the normal full-page reader immediately.

## Additional Context

WX99 implemented the current playback settings popover and the device-local `lingocafe.reader.playback-preferences.v1` model. YI11 established the sentence catalog, active sentence ID, sentence/word highlighting, guided scrolling, seeking, and cross-page playback behavior.

The sentence wrappers in `BookPageReader.tsx` already expose stable playback sentence IDs. This task should derive presentation from the canonical playback order and active sentence rather than introducing a second notion of playback progress.

Refinement selected `Reveal as you listen` and `Dim previous sentences` as the final user-facing labels.

## Assumptions

- Both focus settings preserve the current reader experience when disabled.
- Both focus settings default to off so new, missing, malformed, and legacy preferences preserve the current reader appearance.
- The settings are local playback preferences and persist on the current browser/device like the existing playback settings.
- The active sentence itself is never hidden or dimmed.
- Progressive reveal applies to all rendered content after the active sentence on the active reader page; cross-page behavior follows the existing playback page transition.
- Sentence classification is derived from canonical playback order and active index, not session history.
- Pausing playback keeps the current focus presentation in place.
- Closing, completing, failing, or losing playback availability restores the full page and normal contrast.

## Constraints

- Place both controls in the existing Playback settings popover opened from the gear-and-speed trigger.
- Keep both options independent and apply changes immediately without a Save action.
- Use the existing playback sentence catalog, active sentence ID, and ordering as the source of truth.
- Do not change speech sequencing, seeking, playback percentage, guided scrolling, page transitions, or `audio.play` analytics.
- Do not conflate played-text dimming with existing sentence highlighting, word highlighting, or translation highlighting.
- The active sentence and active word treatments must remain legible when either focus option is enabled.
- Use theme-aware visual styling and avoid unreadable dimming in light, dark, and system themes.
- Visually hide all content after the current sentence in reading order, including later structural and non-sentence content, while preserving the complete page geometry.
- Hidden future content must be absent from pointer interaction, keyboard navigation, and the accessibility tree until revealed, but its layout space must remain stable.
- Turning playback off or disabling progressive reveal must restore all page content without losing the reader’s normal scroll/progress behavior.
- Progressive reveal must reuse normal sentence-centering and must not introduce a separate scroll path, dynamic padding, or layout-height changes.
- Enabling or disabling progressive reveal must not produce an uncontrolled scroll jump or lose the active sentence position.
- Persist and defensively sanitize the new settings through the existing local playback-preference flow; missing and legacy values must resolve safely.
- Support desktop and mobile reader surfaces.
- Run `npm run qa` and visually verify the focus combinations through the LingoCafe test reader.
